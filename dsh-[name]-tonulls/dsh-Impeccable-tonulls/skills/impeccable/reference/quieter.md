# Reduce competing emphasis

Identify the primary action and the information needed to use it. Look for competing accents, heavy borders and shadows, repeated emphasis, and movement that attracts attention away from the task. Reduce those selectively. Preserve contrast, visible focus, state feedback, and the visual cues that distinguish controls from content.

Compare the original and revised screen on the primary task. Check that users can still identify the main action, secondary options, and errors at a glance.
